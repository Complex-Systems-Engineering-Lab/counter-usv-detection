# Evaluation Metrics

Precise definitions of every metric reported in this project. Prior work in this area is hampered by inconsistent attack-success definitions; this document fixes every success condition so results are reproducible and comparable.

**Research questions (three).** RQ1 = attack feasibility (evasion vs. disguise). RQ2 = defense against disguise. RQ3 = adaptive-adversary cost. Marine-EOT robustness and black-box transfer are **conditions on RQ1**, not separate questions.

## Detector performance
- **Clean mAP** — mean average precision on clean (unattacked) EO evaluation data, COCO-style (mAP@[.5:.95] primary, mAP@.5 reported). Computed with `pycocotools`.

## Attack success (RQ1 — attack feasibility)
Both metrics below feed **RQ1**, which measures and **compares** the feasibility of the two attack routes against the raw EO detector. They have different success conditions, so the comparison is reported as a *relative* path-of-least-resistance statement, not a single scalar. Both are scored under the marine-EOT distribution and at white / grey / black-box access levels so feasibility is not a white-box artifact.
- **Evasion success rate (ESR)** — the evasion-route feasibility metric. Fraction of attacked instances where the target's true-class detection is suppressed below the detection confidence threshold (a contact detected when clean becomes undetected). Report the confidence/IoU thresholds used. Scope: full non-detection only (partial evasion / kinematic-layer track-starvation are out of scope).
- **Targeted-misclassification success rate (TMSR)** — the disguise-route feasibility metric. An attacked hostile instance counts as a success **iff**: (a) the contact is still detected (box IoU ≥ 0.5 with ground truth), **and** (b) the predicted class is a *benign* class (fishing / recreational), **and** (c) the clean prediction on the same instance was the correct *hostile* class. This isolates hostile→benign flips from generic label noise.

## Defense performance (RQ2 — defense against a benign label)
The reported defense is **class–motion consistency**: the camera's class against motion measured on an independent track. AIS reports stand in for radar. Two single-feature checks are the operating defense. Comparison scorers (Gaussian mixture, Mahalanobis distance, isolation forest) use the fuller feature sets on the same two arms and the same calibration.

- **Speed-cutoff check** (track only) — flags when the 95th percentile of reported speed over the scored window exceeds the claimed class's cutoff. Windows are the longest complete horizon among 120, 180, and 300 s. This check is not limited to the engagement ring.
- **Closing-rate check** (asset-relative) — flags when the 90th percentile of closing rate toward the protected asset, over the inbound leg, exceeds the claimed class's cutoff. Windows are 300 and 600 s. Features are defined only inside the 6 nm ring (`docs/ENGAGEMENT_GEOMETRY.md`). On a straight run-in, closing rate is about the speed, so a direct-run result is the gap between the benign closing-rate quantile and the benign speed quantile.
- **Either check** — the union of the two frozen checks. A replay flags at the earlier of the two first flags. No new cutoff is fit. The false-alarm denominator is encounters either check can score.
- **Comparison feature arms** — `kinematics_only` adds speed median and spread, loiter fraction, straightness, acceleration, and course statistics. `kinematics_geometry` adds those track features plus bearing stability, closest approach, and the fraction of the window spent closing.

- **Defense detection rate** — fraction of replays flagged at a checkpoint at or after the commit (see the walk-forward section). The headline condition is a **perfect-disguise oracle**: the defense is told the contact carries the target benign class, with no patch. That condition stands in for physical disguise. The tested patches produced no hostile-to-benign label change, so there is no patch-conditioned defense table.
- **False-alarm rate (FAR)** — fraction of **clean, benign** contacts the defense incorrectly flags as inconsistent. Report on held-out AIS tracks. For the geometry arm, report FAR as a **distribution over the locked asset-placement policy** (never a single asset). Video-derived non-cooperative FAR is **conditional** on a defensibly world-projected SMD subset; otherwise retain AIS carriage bias as an unresolved limitation (v1 does not require a dedicated SMD FAR campaign).
- The DDR/FAR tradeoff is reported as a curve over the consistency-score threshold (the "at what false-alarm cost" question).
- **Per-class readout** — detection and false alarms for each claimed class on each check. Fishing and sailing often use the pooled cutoff because they have fewer than 50 scoreable validation encounters. A one-cutoff rescore assigns that pooled cutoff to every class and is how class conditioning is separated from the feature itself. Real-track label-swap is a separate end-of-window check of the full feature models, not a second sequential rate.

## Walk-forward evaluation on replays (RQ2 and RQ3)
- **Replay** — the reported attacker. A held-out real AIS approach is followed until it first comes inside a **commit range** \(R_\mathrm{commit}\) (**nm**); a synthetic run-in to 0.3 nm then replaces the rest of the track. The straight run-in holds the bearing to the asset, at the class 95th-percentile speed (class-plausible), a fixed speed (10, 15, or 20 kn), or a hull burst speed. Commits of 0.5–4 nm retain the recorded approach until the craft is close; commits of 6 and 12 nm are the **sprint**, beginning at the ring edge or at the start of the encounter. Everything before the commit is the recorded benign track.
- **Off-axis run-in** — the same test sources and commits, with only the post-commit segment changed (`configs/attacks/replay_offsets.yaml`). A **capped leg** holds a fixed angle off the bearing to the asset and is re-aimed every step, so closing rate stays near speed times the cosine of that angle; `_t1` variants turn straight in at 1 nm. A **dog-leg** runs straight to a point 1 or 2 nm abeam, then turns in; commits at or inside that abeam distance are skipped. Turns are limited to 8 deg/s. Cutoffs are not refit. A closing-rate score whose window still includes the recorded approach is not a detection of the off-axis leg. Dog-legs at 1–2 nm abeam still close quickly and are a weak evasion test.
- **Arrival bound** — if closing rate stays below a cutoff \(c\), time from range \(R\) to 0.3 nm is at least \((R-0.3)/c\). Staying under both the speed cutoff and the closing-rate cutoff lengthens the approach. A late turn can restore closing-rate detection while collapsing warning.
- **Walk-forward rule** — checkpoints every 60 s; a contact flags at the first checkpoint whose feature exceeds its class cutoff (comparison scorers: whose exceedance passes the calibrated threshold). Cutoffs sit at the validation 5% any-time false-alarm point. A class with fewer than 50 scoreable validation encounters uses the arm-level cutoff; that thin-class fallback does not change a class that already has its own cutoff. A separate class-agnostic routing assigns the arm-level cutoff to every class, including those with enough validation encounters for a cutoff of their own. Nothing is tuned on attacks. Comparison scorers were scored on the straight run-ins.
- **Detection** — fraction of replays flagged at a checkpoint at or after the commit. A replay flagged only on the real prefix is not detected; the any-flag rate is kept as `detect_any`, and a stricter rate drops run-in flags that the benign walk of the same trip and asset also raises at the same checkpoint (`runin_check`).
- **Flag before the commit** — a flag on the real prefix. It is a **false alarm**, not detection of the imitation, and is compared with the close-approach false-alarm rate at the same range, matched on class.
- **Warning (first flag)** — minutes of travel remaining at the first flag, \(t_\mathrm{end}-t_\mathrm{flag}\); a miss counts as zero. Because the first flag may fall on the prefix, this is not a measure of how early the attack is seen.
- **Warning after the commit** — the same quantity from the first flag at or after the commit; a miss counts as zero. It is bounded by the **run-in duration** \(t_\mathrm{end}-t_\mathrm{commit}\approx(R_\mathrm{commit}-0.3)/v_\mathrm{run}\), reported beside it. Information arrives only when AIS reports, so both warnings are lower bounds.
- **Close-approach false-alarm rate** — on held-out benign encounters that come within range \(c\) of the asset, the fraction flagged at a checkpoint before the first checkpoint whose latest report is inside \(c\) (\(c\) = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 12 nm). This is the benign counterpart of a replay flag before a commit at \(c\). The any-time rate on the same encounters is reported beside it.
- **Commit-range readout (primary cost axis)** — detection, warning after the commit, and run-in duration per commit range, 0.5–12 nm, per class and arm. The attacker's cost is the warning conceded: a later commit buys a shorter run-in. The parametric added-approach-time curve \(\Delta t_\mathrm{add}=R(1/v_\mathrm{mimic}-1/v_\mathrm{max})\) on the synthetic sweep is retained as an audit record and is not reported.
- **Report-spacing check** — the same test replays with run-in reports spaced by the training-data report intervals instead of the source track's.
- **Intervals** — 95% Wilson score intervals with the number of distinct source trips as the sample size. Replays from one trip share a prefix across commits and hulls, so counting each trip once is conservative. The class-routing comparison instead reports the paired detection difference on the same replays, with a 95% interval from a bootstrap that resamples source trips and keeps every replay belonging to a drawn trip.
- Reported **per asserted class** and **per check**, under the **perfect-disguise oracle**. The tested patches produced no benign label, so defense rates are not multiplied by a patch success rate.

## Latency
- **Time-to-flag vs. false-alarm** — defined as DDR/FAR vs observation-window length. For v1 this is **not a separate campaign**; it is folded into the RQ3 warning-time / cost-curve readout above.

## Robustness (RQ1 condition)
- **Marine-EOT survival** — attack success as a function of each marine-EOT transform axis (scale, rotation, motion blur, glare, spray, grazing angle, sea state); reported from existing severity tables (aggregate and per-axis where already computed). This is a **robustness condition on RQ1**, not a separate research question — no new per-axis attack×defense campaign is required for v1.

## Transfer (RQ1 condition)
- **Transfer slice** — attack success when optimized on a surrogate and evaluated on a held-out target family; reported separately and explicitly as a thin slice (see `TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md`). This is the **black-box access condition on RQ1**, not a separate research question.

## Baseline comparisons
Defense metrics are reported for the speed-cutoff check, the closing-rate check, their union, and the comparison scorers on both feature arms, and for:
- **Presence-only cross-check** (track exists but no EO detection) — the existing-fusion-defense equivalent.
- **PercepGuard-style supervised bounding-box-sequence classifier** — the closest prior art, but **not measured** in v1, and the two axes on which this defense differs from it are separated by design argument rather than by ablation. Three reasons, all stated as limitations rather than results:
  - *No corpus can pose the head-to-head.* The available video corpus has no `usv`, `fishing`, or `recreational` tracks and no hostile tracks, so the disguise pairings this work is about cannot be posed; the only scorable label swap (cargo vs. small craft) is separable at clip-disjoint AUC ≈ 0.97, which puts any defense at ceiling.
  - *A projected image-plane substitute would be circular.* Apparent target size requires vessel length, which the AIS corpus does not carry (`scripts/data/ingest_ais.py` ingests position, speed, course, heading, ship-type code and transceiver class only). Size could therefore come only from a per-class constant, which would make the range-dependence of image-plane features a property of the projection rather than a measurement. The evidence used instead comes from real detector boxes on real video: apparent loiter fraction separates cargo from small craft at Cohen's *d* ≈ 2.8, and apparent loiter is largely a function of range and camera geometry.
  - *A supervised-vs-one-class detection rate would measure the evaluation, not the defense.* With no hostile class available to train, a supervised class-vs-track check must detect the attack as predicted-vs-asserted class mismatch — a relative decision that always names some benign class, so the measured rate is set by the asserted-class policy chosen by the evaluator. A one-class envelope per class instead bounds each class absolutely, which is what makes a passing track pay in approach speed or geometry; the pooled-envelope ablation quantifies what removing those per-class bounds costs.
- **APRICOT-style anomaly / patch-detection defense** — **deferred** for v1 (not required).

## Reporting conventions
- Report the confidence and IoU thresholds used for all detection and attack-success metrics.
- Report results per detector model rather than aggregating across architectures.
- Report defense metrics under the **perfect-disguise-oracle** condition. State which check (speed, closing rate, either, or a named comparison scorer). Do not invent a patch-conditioned defense table from a relabeling rate of zero.
- Report FAR on AIS-held-out tracks. Video-derived non-cooperative FAR is conditional on a defensibly world-projected SMD subset; otherwise retain AIS carriage bias as an unresolved limitation rather than implying it has been bounded.
- For the geometry arm, state the asset-placement policy (`configs/defense/engagement_geometry.yaml`) and report FAR as a distribution over placements.
- Hostile and adaptive trajectories are synthesized at evaluation only; the benign-behavior scorer is never trained on them. State this wherever DDR is reported.
